Anatomy of a geostationary satellite — a close-up (inspector) view
⚠ You could NOT see this from the ground: at 36,000 km a satellite spans only ~0.2″, far below the ~1″ atmospheric blur — it’s just a point. This is what an inspector spacecraft flying alongside it would see.
In the satellite’s own frame: the dish stays locked on Earth while the solar panels rotate on their actuation axis (which runs North–South, parallel to Earth’s spin axis) to keep facing the Sun as it circles the sky once per day. Star-tracker cameras photograph the background stars to know which way the satellite points.
☀ Sun-tracking (satellite frame)
The Sun (yellow) circles once per day. Watch the blue panels rotate about the N–S axis to stay face-on to it. Edge-on = no power; face-on = full power.
