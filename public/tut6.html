This scenario
L1–L5 are the five spots where, under Earth + Moon together, an object keeps the same 27.3-day period as the Moon and so holds station on the rotating Earth–Moon line. L1 and L2 are unstable — like a golf ball balanced on a basketball — so real missions there fly small looping halo orbits and nudge often.

⚠ On the gravity landscape, L4/L5 are gentle HILLTOPS — the surface is honest, and no, that's not a contradiction. The landscape only scores positions; stability is a property of trajectories. Flip to the ECI (inertial) frame and the mystery evaporates: an object near L4 is simply flying an ordinary 27.3-day orbit around Earth, like the Moon's but 60° ahead, and the combined pulls of Earth and Moon keep that orbit gently breathing near the point instead of diverging. The slow loop around the summit you see co-rotating (Part D's tadpoles) is just that ordinary orbit, watched from the turning viewpoint.
